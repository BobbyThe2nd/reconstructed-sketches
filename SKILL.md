# Reconstructed Sketches

Transform a supplied image into a quiet, hand-drawn observational study. Preserve the subject's recognizable structure, but reconstruct its space rather than copying the photograph.

## Priority Order

When instructions conflict, preserve these priorities in order:

1. Recognizable subject geometry
2. Controlled unconventional spatial construction
3. Geometric simplification
4. Handmade drawing character
5. Restrained color and shading
6. Surface texture and fine detail

Never sacrifice recognizability merely to make the perspective more distorted.

## Analyze the Source

Before transforming the image:

- Identify the primary subject and omit incidental surroundings.
- Reduce it to its most recognizable structural features and major volumes.
- Determine which faces, openings, supports, roof forms, wheels, or repeated modules communicate its identity.
- Treat texture, reflections, seams, labels, and tiny decorative detail as optional unless they are essential to recognition.

For buildings, identify the main mass, roof geometry, facade planes, entrances, and window rhythm. For objects, identify the dominant body, secondary volumes, supports, openings, handles, wheels, and repeated elements.

## Reduce Geometry

Rebuild complex forms with simple primitives:

- Cuboids and prisms
- Flat planes and wedges
- Cylinders
- Simplified curves

Prefer structural clarity to optical realism. Do not reproduce every surface transition, brick, reflection, panel line, or material effect.

## Reconstruct Perspective

Do not automatically enforce one-, two-, or three-point perspective. Treat each major plane as able to follow its own controlled projection system.

Use one or more of the following deliberately:

- Multiple incompatible vanishing points
- Vanishing points inside the image, at the side, or apparently in front of the subject
- Edges that remain parallel beside edges that converge or diverge
- Near elements slightly smaller than corresponding distant elements
- Distant faces slightly enlarged
- Independent rotations or projections for adjacent planes
- Uneven depth compression
- Slightly unbalanced top-to-bottom or side-to-side proportions

Keep the result coherent. The intended feeling is: “I recognize this object immediately, but the space behaves strangely.” Do not turn the subject into random cubism, extreme abstraction, or a visual puzzle that obscures its identity.

## Preserve Structural Rhythm

Keep repeated architectural and industrial features recognizable, but simplify them. Approximate windows, doors, columns, wheels, drawers, vents, and shelves as rhythms rather than exact photographic copies. Small irregularities are welcome.

## Draw Like a Constructed Study

Render forms as a physical, hand-built drawing. Use:

- Graphite-like outlines with slightly uneven pressure
- Visible construction and alignment lines
- Faint extension lines beyond the object
- Occasional double-drawn contours and redraw marks
- Gentle line wobble and imperfect joins
- Partial erasure traces
- Colored-pencil or graphite grain

Avoid perfectly clean vector edges, smooth digital airbrushing, or CAD-like precision. Do not erase every construction line.

## Use Descriptive, Not Photographic, Shading

Do not simulate cinematic lighting. Avoid glossy reflections, high dynamic range, realistic ambient occlusion, ray-traced light, or dramatic cast shadows.

Describe volume with pencil hatching, sparse cross-hatching, gentle tonal blocks, flat colored-pencil regions, and modest local darkening. Keep large areas of paper visible.

## Choose a Restrained Color System

Choose one mode:

### Muted Contrast

Use two to four dominant colors. Keep them low-saturation, dusty, slightly gray, matte, and pencil-like. Suitable pairings include sage and brick, dusty blue and ochre, olive and muted red, gray-blue and warm brown, or pale green and charcoal.

### Monochrome

Use graphite or a single muted hue with several tonal values. Keep the paper color present.

Never use neon, highly saturated color, glossy gradients, or a rainbow palette.

## Compose With Negative Space

Place one clear subject against blank or off-white paper. Keep the background minimal: faint construction marks or a very simple ground reference are enough. Do not reconstruct the photographed environment unless it is necessary to identify the subject.

## Final Check

Before completing the image, verify that:

- The original subject remains immediately recognizable.
- Perspective has been intentionally reconstructed, not accidentally broken.
- Spatial inconsistencies feel controlled.
- Unnecessary photographic detail has been removed.
- The result looks hand drawn rather than vector-rendered or photorealistic.
- The palette is restrained and the shading is descriptive.
- The composition retains generous negative space.
