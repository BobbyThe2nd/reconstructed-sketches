# Examples

## Example 1 — Row house

**Brief:** A small row house, immediately recognizable by its gabled roof, door, and three windows. Reconstruct the facade as a long prism while the roof follows a different vanishing system. Reveal a thin second side plane beside the doorway. Make the far window fractionally larger than the near window. Use gray-blue, faded brick, and graphite.

**Check:** The house should read first; the odd roof/facade relationship should read second.

## Example 2 — Wooden chair

**Brief:** A simple wooden chair reduced to seat box, back plane, and four supports. Give the seat and back incompatible but gentle convergence. Expose a narrow underside plane under the seat and make a rear leg appear slightly more prominent than the front. Draw redrawn graphite edges and sparse ochre shading.

**Check:** It must still be a chair with a believable seating surface.

## Example 3 — Compact van

**Brief:** A compact delivery van with clear cab, cargo box, two visible wheels, and windshield. Use a locally rotated cargo box, a slightly enlarged rear wheel, and a shallow extra hood facet. Keep reflections as flat pale blocks. Render in dusty blue, warm brown, and charcoal.

**Check:** Wheel contact and driving direction stay clear despite the inversion.

## Example 4 — Desk lamp

**Brief:** A task lamp simplified into base disc, articulated arms, and conical shade. Use separate projection systems for the base and shade; turn the shade opening into a subtly faceted ellipse. Keep the page mostly blank with graphite and muted olive.

**Check:** The arm joints explain the odd alignment rather than conceal it.

## Review checklist

- Can a viewer identify the subject in under two seconds?
- Are at least two spatial decisions clearly intentional?
- Are construction lines present but subordinate?
- Is the palette muted and is the background quiet?
- Does the drawing avoid every item in [NEGATIVE_RULES.md](../docs/guides/NEGATIVE_RULES.md)?
