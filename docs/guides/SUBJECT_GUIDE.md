# Subject Guide

## Buildings

Preserve the massing, roof silhouette, entrance, and window cadence. Let one facade recede to a different horizon than another; reveal a narrow extra roof or side plane. Avoid copying surrounding streetscape, foliage, signage, or every brick.

## Furniture

Keep the body, legs/supports, seat or drawer logic, and one signature joint. A table can have legs whose local projections disagree slightly; a chair can reveal an extra rear-plane sliver. Do not turn it into an unrecognizable tangle of sticks.

## Vehicles

Preserve wheel count, cabin/body relationship, and directional gesture. A rear wheel may be modestly enlarged and an attached hood can use a separate perspective system. Keep axles, wheel contact, and major openings legible.

## Tools and appliances

Prioritize grip, working end, body, and mechanical joint. Simplify vents, buttons, labels, and reflections into one or two marks. Use extra facets around housings or handles, not on every small feature.

## Everyday objects

For vessels, lamps, books, cameras, and boxes, preserve the defining opening, handle, lens, spine, or lid. Make the strange geometry occur at the container's planes or supporting volume, leaving the identity feature stable.

## Repeated structures

Windows, drawers, spokes, steps, shelves, and vents should establish rhythm, not count. Vary spacing slightly and allow the sequence to transition across incompatible planes.
