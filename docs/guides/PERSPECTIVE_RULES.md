# Perspective Rules

## Intent

Construct space as an informed, hand-drawn interpretation—not a camera trace. Each change must make the subject more intriguing while leaving it immediately identifiable.

## Spatial interventions

Choose two to four per image; do not activate every rule at once.

1. **Independent plane systems.** Give the main mass, top plane, and attached volume separate horizon logic. Their edge families may converge toward different, implied vanishing points.
2. **Extra-face reveal.** Let a normal box expose a thin additional side or underside plane. It should read as a deliberate folded facet, not a missing contour.
3. **Conflicting convergence.** Allow one edge family to converge while a neighboring parallel-looking family remains parallel or diverges slightly.
4. **Reversed scale cue.** Make a distant repeated part 5–15% larger than its near counterpart, or compress the near portion. Keep overlaps clear so the inversion reads intentionally.
5. **Local rotation.** Rotate an attached door, roof, drawer, wheel, or support a few degrees independently of the body it joins.
6. **Depth compression and expansion.** Flatten one bay while expanding another; use a change in plane width rather than a random bend in an outline.
7. **Split horizon.** Let upper construction lines imply one horizon and lower lines another. The discrepancy should be subtle enough to discover rather than announce itself.

## Coherence constraints

- Keep the silhouette, key openings, supports, and repeated rhythm recognizable.
- Anchor the subject with a few true overlaps and contact points.
- Make distortions consistent within each local plane.
- Use construction lines to explain ambiguous geometry.
- Prefer a sequence of plausible facets over melted or elastic contours.

## Suggested distortion budget

| Subject complexity | Major interventions | Minor interventions |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Simple object | 2 | 1–2 |
| Furniture / vehicle | 2–3 | 2–4 |
| Building | 3–4 | 3–6 |

## Final test

At thumbnail size, name the subject. At normal size, identify at least two intentional spatial inconsistencies. If it reads as a mistake rather than a chosen system, reduce the distortion or reinforce its construction lines.
