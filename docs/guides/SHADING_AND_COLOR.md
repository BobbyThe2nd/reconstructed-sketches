# Shading and Color

## Lighting approach

Describe planes; do not render a photoreal lighting scene. Assign each major face a light, middle, or dark value, then reinforce only the most useful recesses with pencil hatching.

## Value rules

- Leave 35–60% of the paper unmarked.
- Use three to five value steps maximum.
- Save the darkest marks for openings, contact seams, and a few overlaps.
- Cast shadows may be abbreviated into light grounding strokes or omitted.
- Do not use glossy highlights, cinematic contrast, ambient-occlusion haze, or smooth gradients.

## Palette modes

| Mode | Colors | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Graphite study | warm gray, charcoal, paper | architectural or technical quietness |
| Dusty complement | sage + muted brick + graphite | clear plane separation |
| Cool workshop | gray-blue + faded ochre + brown | tools, vehicles, furniture |
| Single hue | muted indigo or olive plus paper | compact, diagrammatic output |

## Application

Lay color in uneven pencil-like blocks. Allow small gaps, crossovers, and visible grain. Color may ignore literal local color when it better separates adjacent planes. Keep the background blank or nearly blank.

## Paper

Use off-white, pale cream, or quiet gray paper. Texture is a supporting signal, never a distressed overlay or a substitute for drawing.
